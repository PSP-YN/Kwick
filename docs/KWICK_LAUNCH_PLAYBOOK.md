# Kwick — Bengaluru Pilot Launch Playbook

**Status: operating plan, not a claim that the service is live.** The customer and partner apps are being separated. Provider supply, legal review, customer support staffing, payments and field operations must be ready before public booking is enabled.

## 1. Pilot objectives

| Measure | Initial target | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Customers | 100 | First real customers with completed onboarding or a completed booking; report each separately |
| Service partners | 30 | 30 onboarded providers is not the same as 30 verified and dispatch-ready providers |
| Launch neighbourhoods | 2–3 | Only activate areas with confirmed provider coverage, service capacity and an operations contact |
| First service categories | Plumbing and electrical | These are the only seeded active categories today |

The database currently has one active service area: HSR Layout, Bengaluru, pincode 560102. The other one or two neighbourhoods are deliberately not named or enabled yet. Choose them after partner conversations and field verification; do not display Koramangala or another area as covered until its service-area row and verified provider coverage exist.

## 2. Provider supply before customer promotion

1. Use existing contacts to recruit local independent professionals and reputable shops.
2. Explain the operating model, service categories, working hours, customer expectations, cancellation handling and dispute process before onboarding.
3. Collect only the minimum identity and trade evidence needed for the approved verification process. Do not request Aadhaar biometrics, full Aadhaar numbers or Aadhaar e-KYC payloads.
4. Review identity, phone reachability, current address/service zone, trade competence, references and applicable trade credentials.
5. Where a formal certificate is not available, arrange a documented in-person skills assessment. Electrical work requiring a licence or authorization must not be assigned to an unqualified person.
6. Record the reviewer, date, evidence reviewed, result and any limitations. A provider stays `pending` until an operator approves them; self-declared details do not equal verification.
7. Confirm provider pricing model and any commission agreement separately. Do not activate an unagreed rate.
8. Run a small number of supervised pilot jobs and review punctuality, workmanship, customer communication and complaint handling before increasing dispatch volume.

## 3. Customer acquisition sequence

1. Start only in the two or three neighbourhoods where verified supply can reliably respond.
2. Invite the first 100 customers through apartment associations, residential welfare groups and community managers.
3. Use Instagram, WhatsApp and tightly targeted local advertising. Do not buy broad reach before the supply side can fulfil requests.
4. Offer first-booking discounts and referral rewards only with a fixed budget, expiry, per-user cap and abuse controls. Record discount cost against each completed booking.
5. Collect feedback after each completed job and contact customers with unresolved complaints.
6. Expand across Bengaluru only after fulfilment, safety, support and unit economics are stable in the pilot zones.

## 4. Launch gates

- Verified active partners are available for each advertised service and area, with backup capacity.
- Provider documents are reviewed and the verification decision is auditable.
- Customer quote and any scope changes are confirmed before work starts.
- Cancellation, complaint, refund and safety escalation paths are staffed.
- Privacy notice, customer terms, provider agreement and tax/payment advice have been reviewed for the actual operating model.
- Discounts, acquisition spend and support costs are measured; no launch claim is based on placeholder data.

## 5. Weekly operating scorecard

Track by neighbourhood and service category:

- New customer signups, first bookings and repeat customers.
- Providers invited, onboarded, verified, active and temporarily unavailable.
- Request-to-provider acceptance rate and median time to first response.
- Completion rate, cancellation rate, on-time arrival and rework/complaint rate.
- Average customer quote, final price, provider earnings and discount cost.
- Customer acquisition cost, support cost per completed booking and contribution margin.

Do not expand solely because signup counts look good. Expansion requires dependable supply, safe work, low complaint rates and positive contribution economics.
